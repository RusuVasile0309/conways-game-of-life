## Challenges

**Story 1.2: The two costliest AI failures of the entire build.**

1. **Missing `"baseUrl": "."` in `tsconfig.base.json`.** AI added path aliases (`@conways-game-of-life/*`) but omitted the `baseUrl` that `tsconfig-paths` requires. Every Nx target crashed before doing any real work. Fix: one-line addition. Root cause: AI applied the alias config without applying a documented constraint it should have known.

2. **Dependency cascade misdiagnosis.** After the baseUrl fix, AI saw a pre-existing Playwright peer-dep warning and incorrectly identified it as the crash cause. It upgraded `@playwright/test`, pulled in conflicting Jest sub-packages, then tried to fix those with pnpm overrides — making things progressively worse. The crash had nothing to do with Playwright versions. The lesson: stop AI when it's iterating on the wrong variable and ask it to re-read the raw error.

**Story 1.5: E2E job runs unconditionally.** The AC specified "run only when `apps/web` or `apps/web-e2e` is affected" — this was not implemented. The e2e job runs on every PR. Pragmatic choice; acceptable for a short build. Minor technical debt.

---

## Key Insights

1. **AI config generation needs a second set of eyes on constraints.** The `baseUrl` miss was a case of AI generating syntactically valid config that violated a well-documented semantic constraint. A quick schema-level check after AI-generated tsconfig changes would have caught it in seconds.

2. **Stop AI when it's iterating on the wrong hypothesis.** The cascade was not caused by what AI thought it was caused by. Asking AI to diagnose from the raw error (not from the warning) recovered the situation immediately.

3. **`nx affected` correctness is non-obvious.** The `fetch-depth: 0` + `filter: tree:0` requirement for Git history depth is easy to miss. AI got it right here; worth verifying on future CI changes.
